The Madhava-Leibniz series was an infinite series that was discovered to converge to pi. The series was independently by Indian mathematician Madhava of Sangamagrama in the 14th century and Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz in the 17th century.

The series works by adding and subtracting fractions made from odd numbers, alternating between addition and subtraction. This process gradually approaches the value of pi divided by 4. Accordingly, multiplying the result by 4 results in an approximation of pi. Interestingly, this series is actually the Taylor series expansion of arctangent (inverse function of tangent) evaluated at 1.

Although the series does converge to pi divided by 4, it does so very slowly, requiring many terms to provide a good approximation.
