Based on the Ramanujan-Sato series, the Chudnovsky Algorithm is a powerful formula that calculates pi with extraordinary speed and precision, each iteration adding about 14 new correct decimal digits for pi. It was developed by the Chudnovsky brothers, David and Gregory, in the late 20th century.

This algorithm produces terms that grow more complex, but add to pi incredibly fast, allowing it to reach millions or even billions of digits of pi with relatively few iterations. Its rapid convergence makes it one of the fastest methods, if not the fastest available for computing pi today.

Because of its efficiency and accuracy, the Chudnovsky Algorithm is the algorithm behind the most recent world records. It is leveraged by programs like y-cruncher for calculating pi to reach astonishing levels of precision, with many of these records computing hundreds of trillions of digits of pi over spans of hundreds of days.
