Also known as the Method of Exhaustion, Archimedes' method for approximating pi relied on geometry. He started by inscribing and circumscribing a hexagon around a circle and calculating their perimeters. With each iteration, he doubled the number of sides of these regular polygons.

The perimeter of the inscribed polygon, always less than the circle’s circumference, gradually increased and approached it from below, forming a lower bound. Meanwhile, the perimeter of the circumscribed polygon, always greater than the circumference, decreased and approached it from above, forming an upper bound.

By calculating the perimeters of these polygons, Archimedes effectively “squeezed” the value of pi between these two bounds. He repeated this process until reaching a 96-sided polygon, where the calculations became impractical without modern tools.
